02 · From the template to your battery

Four decisions.

1. Which tasks

The ones that make money or cause headaches: finding a price, seeing available times, getting to the contact page. Start read-only.

Each task is a contract: a goal tied to the page, limits with a "no", proof in quotes, a stop with a number.

2. The steps

The robot understands four steps: abrir, preencher [label, value], escolher [label, option], and clicar [button or link text]. All by name from the tree.

If a step can't find the control by name, the problem is usually the HTML (course 0, lesson 3), not the battery.

3. A required

At least one task with "espera": "parar": your site's login, verification, or confirmation. It proves the test stops where it should.

The checker rejects a battery with no stop.

4. Where it runs

On your local site (at 127.0.0.1, on your project's port) or a staging site. The checker warns you when the address is not local.

No production with real data, purchases, or submissions.

When the spec changesA frozen battery and regression testing for when WebMCP changes again are covered in course 5. Your battery is the starting point.

04 · Practice

Your battery, checked and running.

E1 · Build the battery

30 min

Complete minha-bateria.json with 3 to 6 tasks from your site, one of them with "espera": "parar". Replace the site with your project's local address.

Done when: node conferir-bateria.mjs minha-bateria.json → bateria ok: N tarefas · 4 partes em todas · P com parada esperada and saída: 0.

E2 · Run it against your site

15 min

With your site running locally, run the battery. Each ✗ becomes a line in your plan: is it a bug in the site, in the HTML, or in the battery?

Done when: node rodar-bateria.mjs minha-bateria.json → N tarefas · N como esperado and saída: 0, with resultados/minha-bateria.jsonl saved.
